This section provides essential historical and political notes accompanying Mao Zedong's March 1926 essay. It defines the comprador class (Chinese managers and commercial agents serving foreign imperialist capital), the fascist Étatiste clique, and Dai Jitao's right-wing Kuomintang ideology that prepared the ground for Chiang Kai-shek's 1927 counter-revolutionary coup.

The notes also document the First United Front forged at the Kuomintang's First National Congress in January 1924 under Sun Yat-sen's Three Great Policies (alliance with Soviet Russia, cooperation with the Communist Party, and assistance to workers and peasants), as well as the landmark working-class strikes of 1922 to 1926: the 1922 Hong Kong seamen's strike, the February 7, 1923 Beijing-Hankou railway strike, the Kailan and Jiaozuo coal mine strikes, the 1924 Shamian anti-colonial strike in Guangzhou, and the massive May 30th Movement and sixteen-month Canton-Hong Kong general strike of 1925 to 1926.
