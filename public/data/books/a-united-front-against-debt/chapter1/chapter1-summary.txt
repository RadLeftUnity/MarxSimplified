Delivered on July 29, 1987, at the summit of the Organization of African Unity (OAU) in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, this historic address by Captain Thomas Sankara is one of the most powerful anti-imperialist indictments of sovereign foreign debt in modern history. Sankara demonstrates that Africa's crippling debt burden is not a legitimate financial obligation or moral contract, but rather a direct continuation of colonial exploitation engineered by Western finance capital and former colonial masters to maintain economic domination.

Sankara analyzes debt as a mechanism of neocolonial reconquest that subjugates national development to foreign diktats, turning technically free nations into modern debt slaves. Pointing out the stark material reality that lenders will survive if debts are cancelled while African working masses will face starvation, austerity, and death if debts are paid, he issues a passionate call for an all-African united front against debt repudiation. Warning with prophetic clarity that standing alone against global imperialist finance capital would mean assassination, Sankara urges African leaders to break their reliance on luxury imports, foster continental self-sufficiency, redirect resources to peasant agriculture and domestic industry, and live with dignity as Africans.
