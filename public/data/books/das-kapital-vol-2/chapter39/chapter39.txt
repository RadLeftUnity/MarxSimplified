II. The Working Period Greater than the Period of Circulation

The working and turnover periods of capitals I and II cross one another
instead of relieving one another. Simultaneously some capital is set free.
This was not so in the previously considered case.

But this does not alter the fact that, as before, 1) the number
of working periods of the total capital advanced is equal to the sum of
the value of the annual product of both advanced portions of capital divided
by the total capital advanced, and 2) the number of turnovers made by the
total capital is equal to the sum of the two amounts turned over divided
by the sum of the two advanced capitals. Here too we must consider both
portions of capital as if they performed turnover movements entirely independent
of each other.

Thus, we assume once more, that &pound;100 are to be advanced
weekly to the labour-process. Let the working period last 6 weeks, requiring
therefore every time an advance of &pound;600 (capital I). Let the time
of circulation be 3 weeks, so that the period of turnover is 9 weeks, as
before. Let capital II of &pound;300 step in during the three-week circulation
period of capital I. Considering both capitals as independent of each other,
we find the schedule of the annual turnover to be as follows:

The process of production continues uninterruptedly the whole year round
on the same scale. The two capitals I and II remain entirely separate.
But in order to represent them as separate, we had to tear apart their
real intersections and intertwinings, and thus also to change the number
of turnovers. For according to the above table the amounts turned over
would be:

by capital I, 
5⅔ times 600, or &pound;3,400 and

by capital II, 
5 times 300, or &pound;1,500

Hence by the total capital 5 4/9 times 900, or &pound;4,900.

But this is not correct, for, as we shall see, the actual periods of
production and circulation do not absolutely coincide with those of the
above schedule, in which it was mainly a question of presenting capitals
I and II as independent of each other.

In reality, capital II has no working and circulating periods
separate and distinct from those of capital I. The working period is 6
weeks, the circulation period 3 weeks. Since capital II amounts to only
&pound;300, it can suffice only for a part of the working period. This
is indeed the case. At the end of the 6th week a product valued at &pound;600
passes into circulation and returns in money-form at the close of the 9th
week. Then, at the opening of the 7th week, capital II begins its activity,
and covers the requirements of the next working period, the 7th to 9th
week. But according to our assumption the working period is only half up
at the end of the 9th week. Hence capital I of &pound;600 having just returned,
at the beginning of the 10th week, once more enters into operation and
with its &pound;300 supplies the advances needed for the 10th to 12th week.
This disposes of the second working period. A product value of &pound;600
is in circulation and will return at the close of the 15th week. At the
same time, &pound;300, the amount of the original capital II, are set free
and are able to function in the first half of the following working period,
that is to say, in the 13th to 15th week. After the lapse of these weeks
the &pound;600 return; &pound;300 of them suffice for the remainder of
the working period, and &pound;300 remain for the following working period.

The thing therefore works as follows:

First period of turnover: 1st-9th week.

1st working period: 1st-6th week. Capital I, &pound;600, performs its function.

1st period of circulation: 7th-9th week. End of 9th week, &pound;600 return.

Second period of turnover: 7th-15th week.

2nd working period: 7th-12th week.

First half: 7th-9th week. Capital II, &pound;300, performs its function.

End of 9th week, &pound;600 return in money-form (capital I).

Second half: 10th-12th week. &pound;300 of capital I perform their
function. The other &pound;300 of capital I remain freed.

2nd period of circulation: 13th-15th week.

End of 15th week, &pound;600 (half taken from capital I, half from
capital II) return in the form of money.

Third period of turnover; 13-21st week.

3rd working period: 13th-18th week.

First half: 13th-15th week. The freed &pound;300 perform their function.
End of 15th week, &pound;600 return in money-form.

Second half: 16th-18th week, &pound;300 of the returned &pound;600
function, the other &pound;300 again remain freed.

3rd period of circulation: 19th-21st week at the close of which &pound;600
again return in money-form. In these &pound;600 capital I and capital II
are now indistinguishably fused.

And so there are eight full turnover periods of a capital of &pound;600
(I: 1st-9th week; II: 7th-15th week; III: 13th-21st; IV: 19th-27th; V:
25th-33rd; VI: 31st-39th; VII: 37th-45th; VIII: 43rd-51st week) to the
end of the 51st week. But as the 49th-51st weeks fall within the eighth
period of circulation, the &pound;300 of freed capital must step in and
keep production going. Thus the turnover at the end of the year is as follows:
&pound;600 have completed their circuit eight times, making &pound;4,800.
In addition we have the product of the last 3 weeks (49th-51st), which,
however, has completed only one-third of its circuit of 9 weeks, so that
in the sum turned over it counts for only one-third of its amount, &pound;100.
If, then, the annual product of 51 weeks is &pound;5,100, the capital turned
over is only 4,800 plus 100, or &pound;4,900. The total capital advanced,
&pound;900, has therefore been turned over 5 4/9 times, a trifle more than
in the first case.

In the present example we assumed a case in which the working
time was ⅔ and the circulation time ⅓ of the period of turnover, i.e.,
the working time was a simple multiple of the circulation time. The question
now is whether capital is likewise set free, in the way shown above, when
this assumption is not made.

Let us assume a working time of 5 weeks, a circulation time of 4 weeks,
and a capital advance of &pound;100 per week.

First period of turnover: 1st-9th week.

1st working period: 1st-5th week. Capital I, or &pound;500, performs its function.

1st circulation period: 6th-9th week. End of 9th week, &pound;500 return in
money-form.

Second period of turnover: 6th-14th week.

2nd working period: 6th-10th week.

First section: 6th-9th week. Capital II, of &pound;400, performs its
function. End of 9th week, capital I of &pound;500 returns in money-form.

Second section: 10th week. &pound;100 of the returned &pound;500 perform
their function. The remaining &pound;400 are set free for the following
working period.

2nd circulation period: 11th-14th week. End of 14th week. &pound;500 return
in money-form.

Up to the end of the 14th week (11th-14th), the &pound;400 set free above
perform their function; &pound;100 of the &pound;500 then returned fill
the requirements of the third working period (11th-15th week) so that &pound;400
are once more released for the fourth working period. The same thing is
repeated in every working period; at its beginning &pound;40 are ready
at hand, sufficing for the first 4 weeks. End of the 4th week, &pound;500
return in money-form, only &pound;100 of which are needed for the last
week, while the other &pound;400 remain free for the next working period.

Let us further assume a working period of 7 weeks, with a capital
I of &pound;700; a circulation period of 2 weeks, with a capital II of
&pound;200.

In that case the first period of turnover lasts from the 1st to
the 9th week; its first working period from the 1st to the 7th week, with
an advance of &pound;700, its first circulation period from the 8th to
the 9th week. End of the 9th week, &pound;700 flow back in money-form.

The second period of turnover, from the 8th to the 16th week,
contains the second working period of the 8th to the 14th week. The requirements
of the 8th and 9th weeks of this period are covered by capital II. End
of the 9th week, the above &pound;700 return. Up to the close of this working
period (10th-14th week), &pound;500 of this sum are used up; &pound;200
remain free for the next working period. The second circulation period
lasts from the 15th to the 16th week. End of the 16th week &pound;700 return
once more. From now on, the same thing is repeated in every working period.
The need for capital during the first two weeks is covered by the &pound;200
set free at the close of the preceding working period; at the close of
the second week &pound;700 return; but only 5 weeks remain of the working
period, so that it can consume only &pound;500; therefore &pound;200 always
remain free for the next working period.

We find, then, that in the given case, where the working period
has been assumed to be greater than the circulation period, a money-capital
will at all events have been set free at the close of each working period,
which is of the same magnitude as capital II advanced for the circulation
period. In our three illustrations capital II was &pound;300 in the first,
&pound;400 in the second, and &pound;200 in third. Accordingly, the capital
set free at the close of each working period was &pound;300, &pound;400
and &pound;200 respectively.
