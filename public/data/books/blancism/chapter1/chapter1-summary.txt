Published in Pravda in April 1917, this razor-sharp article by V.I. Lenin attacks the reformist leadership of the Petrograd Soviet: particularly Menshevik and Socialist-Revolutionary leaders such as Chkheidze and Tsereteli. Lenin demonstrates that their conciliatory policies towards the bourgeois Provisional Government directly reproduce the nineteenth-century errors of French socialist Louis Blanc. In 1848, Blanc abandoned the class struggle for petty-bourgeois illusions, imagining that the capitalist state could peacefully organize labor and assist workers.

Lenin scrutinizes the question of imperialist war and annexations. While proletarians maintain uncompromising class hostility to imperialist predation, petty-bourgeois opportunists issue sentimental appeals urging the capitalist regime to renounce annexations in abstract declarations while leaving capitalist rule untouched. Lenin exposes the hypocrisy of liberal Cadet publications regarding territories like Kurland, demanding a universal definition of annexations applied equally to all powers and calling on workers to break completely with bourgeois deception.
