This section breaks down the difference between hostile battles and friendly disagreements:

1. Hostile (Antagonistic) Conflicts: Opposing interests where one side wins by overcoming the other (such as workers resisting oppressive factory conditions or nations fighting invaders).

2. Friendly (Non-Antagonistic) Conflicts: Differences that can be resolved peacefully through discussion, compromise, and constructive criticism (such as teammates debating project strategies).

3. Conflicts Can Escalate: If minor disagreements are ignored or mishandled, they can turn into hostile, destructive fights over time.

Real-life Example: When team members disagree on a project design, it is a non-antagonistic conflict solved through discussion. But if management refuses to pay employees for mandatory overtime, that economic exploitation becomes an antagonistic conflict requiring legal or collective action.
