This chapter contrasts two fundamental ways of viewing the world:

1. The Metaphysical View: Believes things stay basically the same and only change when pushed from the outside. It views issues in isolation (like blaming an employee's low output entirely on poor attitude without looking at broken tools or bad management).

2. The Dialectical View: Believes change is driven primarily from within things. Outside conditions matter, but internal factors decide what actually happens.

Real-life Example: Warmth (an external condition) enables a fertile egg to hatch into a chick, but no amount of warmth will hatch a stone into a chick because the stone lacks the internal biological foundation. Success in work and life requires both the right internal setup and supportive external conditions.
