This chapter explains how to set priorities and recognize shifting power dynamics:

1. The Main Problem (Principal Contradiction): In any complex situation with many issues, one primary conflict controls the rest. Solve that main issue first to make progress on everything else.

2. The Dominant Side (Principal Aspect): Whichever side of a conflict currently holds power determines the current state of affairs.

3. The New Replaces the Old: Power dynamics are not permanent. Over time, an emerging underdog force grows stronger and eventually replaces the old ruling force.

Real-life Example: If a student is failing school because they lack home internet access, buying them fancy notebooks won't solve the problem. Getting internet access is the principal contradiction. Once that is fixed, secondary issues like study schedules can be addressed.
