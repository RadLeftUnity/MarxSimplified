In the second part of the 1892 Introduction, Engels traces the historical rise of the English middle class and the political role of religion in class struggle. He examines three decisive battles: the Protestant Reformation against feudal Catholicism, the English Civil War where Calvinism served as the religious ideological banner for the rising bourgeoisie, and the French Revolution. Engels demonstrates how the bourgeoisie, having initially championed freethought to overthrow feudalism, hastily retreated into religious orthodoxy once the industrial working class emerged as an independent threat. Viewing religion as an essential social pacifier ('religion must be kept alive for the people'), the British and European ruling classes utilized religious institutions to instill docility and maintain class rule.
