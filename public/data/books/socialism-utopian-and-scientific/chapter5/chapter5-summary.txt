Engels expounds the core principles of scientific socialism and historical materialism. He identifies the fundamental contradiction of capitalism: the conflict between socialized production and private capitalist appropriation. While modern industry concentrates thousands of workers into collective, coordinated production, the products are privately seized by individual capitalists. This contradiction manifests as the antagonism between the proletariat and the bourgeoisie, and as the collision between planned organization inside individual enterprises and anarchic competition across society as a whole. As periodic crises of overproduction paralyze the economy, capitalism forces the formation of trusts and state monopolies, demonstrating that the bourgeoisie has become economically redundant. By seizing political power and converting the means of production into public property, the proletariat resolves this contradiction, ending class division. With no class left to suppress, the repressive machinery of the state becomes superfluous and dies out. Society transitions from the kingdom of necessity to the kingdom of freedom.
