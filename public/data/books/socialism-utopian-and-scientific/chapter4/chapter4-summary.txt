Engels contrasts metaphysical thought with dialectical materialism. Metaphysical thinking treats objects and concepts in isolation, as fixed, unchanging, and rigid antitheses (a thing either exists or does not exist). While useful in everyday observation and early natural science, metaphysics fails when examining movement, transformation, and historical development. Dialectics, pioneered in modern philosophy by G.W.F. Hegel, understands the world as an interconnected process of ceaseless change and contradiction. However, because Hegel was an idealist who viewed reality as the unfolding of an 'Absolute Idea', his system had to be turned right side up. Karl Marx achieved this by uniting dialectics with materialism. Engels highlights Marx's two epoch-making discoveries: the materialist conception of history (which explains social consciousness through material conditions) and surplus value (which reveals the hidden secret of capitalist exploitation). With these discoveries, socialism was transformed into a science.
