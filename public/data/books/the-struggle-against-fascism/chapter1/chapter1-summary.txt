Delivered as a landmark report to the Enlarged Executive Committee of the Communist International in June 1923 and published in Labour Monthly in August 1923, Clara Zetkin provides one of the earliest and most penetrating Marxist analyses of fascism. Zetkin refutes both social-democratic reformists who view fascism as mere lawless violence and communists who mistake it for a narrow military clique like Horthy's White Terror in Hungary. She demonstrates that fascism grows out of the structural decay of capitalism and the betrayal of working-class revolution by reformist leaders, recruiting ruined petty-bourgeois strata and disillusioned workers with pseudo-revolutionary demagogy before serving big industrial and finance capital through terror and wage cuts. Zetkin concludes that defeating fascism requires an ideological and political offensive for a Workers' and Peasants' Government alongside an organized proletarian United Front and armed working-class self-defense.
