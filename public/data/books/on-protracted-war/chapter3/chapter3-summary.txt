Mao establishes the objective material basis of the Sino-Japanese war as a life-and-death struggle in the 1930s between semi-colonial, semi-feudal China and imperialist Japan. He identifies four mutually contradictory characteristics on each side: Japan possesses great military, economic, and organizational power, but its war is reactionary and barbarous, its manpower and material resources are inadequate for a long war, and it faces growing international opposition; China is militarily and economically weak, but it is in an era of progress led by the Communist Party, its war is just and progressive, its vast territory and population can sustain a protracted conflict, and it enjoys broad international support.
