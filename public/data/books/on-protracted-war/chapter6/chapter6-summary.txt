Mao synthesizes his critique of both the defeatist theory of national subjugation and the rash theory of quick victory, showing that both stem from philosophical subjectivism and one-sidedness. While subjugationists inflate the single contradiction of military strength versus weakness into the whole picture, quick-victory theorists deny China's material inferiority and mistake a localized tactical victory for the entire war ("a leaf before the eye shuts out Mount Tai"). Taking an objective, all-sided dialectical view, Mao affirms that protracted war is the sole realistic strategy to secure the conditions for liberation.
