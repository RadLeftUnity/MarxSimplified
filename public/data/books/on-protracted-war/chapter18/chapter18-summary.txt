Mao explains how China achieves its strategic objective of exhausting Japan (war of attrition) chiefly through fighting tactical campaigns and battles of annihilation. While positional warfare conducts attrition and mobile warfare conducts annihilation, guerrilla warfare performs both simultaneously; by concentrating superior forces to encircle and annihilate enemy units and combining military blows with lenient, politically conscious treatment of Japanese prisoners, China shatters the arrogance and morale of the imperialist army.
