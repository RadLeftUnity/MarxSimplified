Mao outlines his famous strategic forecast of the three stages of the Sino-Japanese protracted war: first, the period of the enemy's strategic offensive and China's strategic defensive, characterized primarily by mobile warfare; second, the pivotal period of strategic stalemate, where Japan attempts to consolidate its occupied cities and communication lines while Chinese guerrilla warfare across vast rural base areas drains Japanese military and financial resources and reverses the balance of forces; and third, the period of China's strategic counter-offensive and Japan's strategic retreat to recover all lost territories.
