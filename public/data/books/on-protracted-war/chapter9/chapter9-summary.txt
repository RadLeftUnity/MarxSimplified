Mao analyzes the unique interlocking "jig-saw pattern" of the anti-Japanese war resulting from Japan's troop shortage and China's vast territory and progressive rural base areas. Like the game of weichi (Go), the war features simultaneous encirclement and counter-encirclement across four dimensions: interior and exterior lines, possession and non-possession of rear areas, strategic encirclement by Japan versus tactical and base-area counter-encirclement by Chinese guerrilla and regular forces, and enemy control of small urban and railway zones surrounded by vast liberated rural expanses.
