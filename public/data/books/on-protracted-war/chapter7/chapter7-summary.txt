Mao explains why the Sino-Japanese conflict must be protracted by examining the dynamic interplay of all four fundamental contradictions between China and Japan. Because Japan's military superiority and China's military inferiority are relative rather than absolute, Japan's victories and China's defeats in the initial stage are strictly limited in scope. As the war continues under correct political and military tactics, Japan's inherent disadvantages (resource shortages, barbarity, and international isolation) steadily worsen while China's advantages (popular mobilization, vast territory, and international solidarity) steadily grow, gradually reversing the balance of forces.
