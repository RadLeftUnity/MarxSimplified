Mao emphasizes that the progressive transformation of the army and the political mobilization of the masses are the decisive foundations of victory over Japanese imperialism. Because soldiers are the foundation of the army and the masses are the richest source of power to wage war, the anti-Japanese forces must abolish feudal abuses, practice internal democracy, unite closely with the people, and uphold three core political principles: unity between officers and men, unity between the army and the people, and the political disintegration of enemy forces.
