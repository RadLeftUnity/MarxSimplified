Mao formulates three principles governing decisive engagements in the anti-Japanese war: fight a decisive engagement resolutely in every battle or campaign where victory is certain; avoid a decisive engagement wherever victory is uncertain; and absolutely avoid any strategically decisive engagement on which the fate of the entire nation is staked. By trading space for time when necessary and refusing to gamble the nation's survival on a single throw, China foils Japan's plan for a quick decision and preserves its forces for protracted victory.
