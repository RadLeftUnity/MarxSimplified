Mao frames the central questions confronting China ten months into the War of Resistance Against Japan: Will China be subjugated? Can China win quickly? Rejecting both the defeatist "theory of national subjugation" and the impetuous "theory of quick victory" as subjective and one-sided, he reviews his July 1936 interview with Edgar Snow and demonstrates why the war is necessarily protracted, requiring an expansive anti-Japanese national united front, mobile warfare over fluid battlefields, and widespread peasant guerrilla warfare.
