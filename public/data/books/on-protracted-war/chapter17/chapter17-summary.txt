Mao examines the relationship and shifting primacy among the three forms of warfare across the three stages of the War of Resistance: mobile warfare (fluid, regular offensive operations over vast battlefields, primary in the first and third stages), guerrilla warfare (primary in the second stage of stalemate, playing the dual strategic role of supporting regular warfare and transforming itself into regular mobile forces), and positional warfare (supplementary throughout). He rejects both the flightism of "only retreat, never advance" and the desperate recklessness of "only advance, never retreat."
