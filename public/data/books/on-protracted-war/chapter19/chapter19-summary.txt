Mao identifies five major strategic and operational blunders already committed by the Japanese high command during the first ten months of the war: piecemeal reinforcement arising from underestimation of China and troop shortages, absence of a main direction of attack, lack of strategic coordination among rival warlord and capitalist cliques, failure to grasp strategic opportunities, and routing Chinese forces without annihilating them. Furthermore, backed by an organized population, Chinese commanders can actively induce enemy mistakes through feints and deception.
