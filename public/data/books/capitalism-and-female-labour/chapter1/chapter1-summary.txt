Published in Pravda in May 1913, this short essay by V.I. Lenin examines the hidden depths of exploitation suffered by working-class women under modern capitalism. Drawing on evidence from a Paris exhibition that documented the starvation piece-rates paid to female home-workers making lampshades, artificial flowers, and clothing, Lenin illustrates how capitalists prey on impoverished families by driving women into sweated domestic piecework for fractions of a penny per hour.

Lenin directly connects economic wage slavery to the scourge of prostitution, dismissing bourgeois moral posturing as transparent hypocrisy. Throughout history, whether under chattel slavery, feudal serfdom, or industrial capitalism, exploiting classes have coerced oppressed classes into surrendering unpaid labor and providing women as concubines for the wealthy. Lenin calls on socialist trade unions and workers' organizations to publicize these conditions widely, helping both male and female wage-slaves recognize their shared exploitation and unite in revolutionary class struggle.
