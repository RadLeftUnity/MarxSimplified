Commemorating the second anniversary of Soviet power, Lenin contrasts the empty rhetoric of bourgeois democracy with the concrete achievements of the proletarian revolution. While even the most advanced capitalist republics maintain inequalities in marriage, divorce, and property, the Soviet state swept away all legal subordination of women. Lenin emphasizes that legal equality is only the first step: complete liberation requires liberating women from the soul-destroying drudgery of private domestic housework through socialized kitchens, public nurseries, and collective dining rooms, enabling women to participate fully in economic and political life.
