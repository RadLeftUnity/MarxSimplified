Karl Marx's 1845 Theses on Feuerbach consists of eleven foundational aphorisms that establish the cornerstone of dialectical and historical materialism. Marx critiques all previous materialism, including Ludwig Feuerbach's, for treating reality and contemplation passively rather than as sensuous human activity and revolutionary practice.

In Thesis 2, Marx defines truth not as an abstract scholastic question, but as a practical question proven in struggle. In Thesis 3, he refutes the mechanical materialist view that people are merely passive products of circumstances, demonstrating that 'the educator must himself be educated' through revolutionary praxis.

In Thesis 6, Marx shows that human essence is not an abstract biological constant inside isolated individuals, but 'the ensemble of social relations.'

The work culminates in the celebrated Thesis 11: 'The philosophers have only interpreted the world in various ways; the point, however, is to change it.'
