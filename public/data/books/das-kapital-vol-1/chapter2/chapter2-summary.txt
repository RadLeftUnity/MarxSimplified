Chapter Overview: Commodities

Marx analyzes the commodity as the fundamental unit of capitalist wealth. He establishes that commodities possess a dual character: use-value (their concrete utility) and exchange-value (their quantitative ratio in market exchange). Value itself is defined by the socially necessary labor-time required for production. Marx introduces the dual character of labor (concrete labor producing use-value and abstract labor producing value) and concludes with his famous analysis of Commodity Fetishism, explaining how social relations between people under capitalism falsely present themselves as physical relations between things.
