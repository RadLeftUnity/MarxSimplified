Among the many problems that demand the consideration and attention of contemporary mankind, sexual problems are undoubtedly some of the most crucial. Mankind today is living through an acute sexual crisis which is far more unhealthy and harmful for being long and drawn-out.

The conservatives argue that we should return to the past and re-establish the old foundations of the family. The champions of bourgeois individualism say that we ought to destroy all hypocritical restrictions. Socialists assure us that sexual problems will only be settled when the basic reorganisation of the social and economic structure of society has been tackled.

The history of human society gives us the clue to finding the magic thread. It isn't the first time that mankind has gone through a sexual crisis. The "sexual crisis" was particularly acute at the time of the Renaissance and the Reformation, when a great social advance pushed feudal nobility into the background and cleared the way for the rising bourgeoisie.

The sexual morality of feudalism developed out of tribal authoritativeness and collective patrimonial life. Under capitalism, the triumphant principles of individualism and exclusive private property destroyed whatever remained of community life.

Today, the sexual crisis does not spare even the working class or the peasantry. The waves of the sexual crisis are sweeping over the threshold of workers' homes, creating situations of conflict that concern the working class in its daily life. One of the tasks that confront the working class in its attack on the future is the task of establishing healthier and more joyful relationships between the sexes.
