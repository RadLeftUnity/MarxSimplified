In this address delivered on April 21, 1986, at the opening of the International Symposium for the Creation of the Black Institute (Institut des peuples noirs) in Ouagadougou, Captain Thomas Sankara presents a Pan-African framework for cultural decolonization, historical responsibility, and universal human solidarity. Sankara begins by paying homage to Senegalese historian Cheikh Anta Diop, arguing that true tribute consists not of passive tears or grief, but of continuing Diop's scientific and political combat with unyielding courage and rigor.

Sankara examines the shared historical trauma and heroic resistance uniting African peoples and the Black diaspora across the world: centuries of chattel slavery, colonial subjugation, apartheid, and ongoing imperialist plunder. He challenges the symposium to establish the Black Institute as a living crucible of research, cultural preservation, and creative rebirth. Emphasizing that reclaiming African historical agency is not an exercise in racial insularity or narrow chauvinism, Sankara insists that the Institute must remain open to all peoples: true cultural self-determination is the essential foundation from which formerly oppressed nations contribute as equals to universal civilization and collective human emancipation.
