Written in late 1915 during the First World War, this essay by V.I. Lenin critiques the left-wing Polish socialist Karl Radek (writing under the pseudonym Parabellum) for rejecting the slogan of the right of nations to self-determination. Lenin argues that Radek wrongly looks only backward to Western European nation-state formation rather than forward to the burgeoning anti-colonial liberation movements across Asia, Africa, and the global periphery.

Lenin clarifies that imperialism heightens national oppression on an unprecedented global scale. Therefore, the socialist revolution must not be contraposed to democratic struggles, but combined with an uncompromising fight for all democratic rights: including the right of oppressed nations to political secession. Socialists in dominant oppressor nations must demand the right of secession for oppressed peoples, while socialists in oppressed nations must champion working-class unity across borders. Lenin emphasizes that Marx's support for Irish independence demonstrated how the freedom of an oppressed nation is a prerequisite for the revolutionary liberation of the working class in the oppressor nation itself.
