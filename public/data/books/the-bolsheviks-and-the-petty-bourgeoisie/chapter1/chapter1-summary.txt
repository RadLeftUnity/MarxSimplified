In this 1907 polemic published in Novy Luch, V.I. Lenin clarifies the Marxist analysis of class differentiation in the Russian revolutionary upheaval. Replying to the Trudovik newspaper Noviye Sily, Lenin dissects the social foundation of the Constitutional-Democratic Party (Cadets), demonstrating that they represent liberal landlords, capitalist agricultural estate owners, and bourgeois intellectuals who dread peasant agrarian revolt and proletarian solidarity.

Lenin refutes the Menshevik dogma that because the economic content of the revolution is bourgeois, the liberal bourgeoisie must lead it. He explains that a bourgeois revolution can either be directed by liberal exploiters who compromise with monarchist reaction or by the proletariat in militant alliance with the peasant petty bourgeoisie. To defeat tsarism and eradicate landlordism, the workers' party must systematically liberate the peasant masses from liberal hegemony, adopting the tactical formula of marching separately while striking together against both reaction and liberal capitulation.
