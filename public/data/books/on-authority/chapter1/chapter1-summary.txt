Friedrich Engels refutes the abstract, moralistic opposition to authority advanced by anarchists and autonomists. By analyzing the physical realities of modern industrial production, automated textile mills, railways, and maritime navigation, Engels demonstrates that large-scale collective labor inevitably requires coordination, division of tasks, and subordination to technical schedules regardless of the prevailing social system.

Engels further exposes the fallacy of demanding the immediate abolition of political authority and the state on day one of a revolution. A revolution is itself the most authoritarian act possible, as the working class forcibly imposes its collective will upon the capitalist class using rifles and cannons. Engels concludes that authority and autonomy are dynamic material relationships shaped by production rather than eternal moral absolutes, and that attempts to abolish organizational authority only serve bourgeois reaction.
