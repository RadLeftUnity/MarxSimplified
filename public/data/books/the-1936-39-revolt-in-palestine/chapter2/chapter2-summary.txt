Kanafani analyzes the emergence, composition, and specific contradictions of the Palestinian Arab working class under the British Mandate. He details how British colonial policy prioritized Jewish capital concessions while deliberately stifling independent Arab industrial development. Simultaneously, the Zionist movement implemented its aggressive 'conquest of labor' (Hebrew Labor) policy through the Histadrut, systematically boycotting Arab workers and expelling them from construction sites, orchards, and factories. Facing dual exploitation by British capital and Zionist exclusion, Palestinian Arab workers formed independent militant trade unions, such as the Palestine Arab Workers Society, launching widespread strikes in transport, ports, and municipal services. Kanafani highlights the revolutionary potential of this young working class, while examining how the absence of a vanguard Marxist party left the workers politically subordinated to the reactionary feudal-bourgeois leadership.
