This chapter investigates the political leadership, class dynamics, and ideological weaknesses of the Palestinian national movement prior to 1936. Kanafani analyzes the bitter factional rivalry between dominant aristocratic and bourgeois clans, primarily the Husaynis and the Nashashibis. The traditional elite, represented by the Arab Higher Committee and the Grand Mufti, sought to preserve their socio-economic dominance by pursuing a policy of vacillation: alternating between mild formal petitions to British colonial officials and reluctant endorsements of popular protests only when forced by mass unrest. Meanwhile, the emerging petty-bourgeois intellectuals and student youth grew increasingly radicalized by Zionist expansion, yet remained organizationally unequipped to wrest leadership away from the feudal notables. Kanafani demonstrates how this class contradiction crippled the political direction of the national liberation movement on the eve of the revolt.
