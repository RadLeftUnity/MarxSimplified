Kanafani delivers an exhaustive historical materialist narrative of the 1936-1939 revolt, dividing it into two distinct phases. The first phase began in April 1936 with a historic six-month general strike, sustained by spontaneous popular national committees and rural guerrilla actions. Alarmed by the revolutionary momentum, the feudal Arab Higher Committee conspired with neighboring British-aligned Arab monarchs (Transjordan, Iraq, Saudi Arabia) to call off the strike under false British promises. The publication of the 1937 Peel Commission report, which proposed the partition of Palestine, reignited the second phase: a full-scale armed peasant insurrection that liberated vast rural districts and major towns from British control. Britain responded with massive military terror, collective punishment, village demolitions, and martial law, while arming and training Zionist paramilitary units (the Special Night Squads). Kanafani concludes with an incisive balance sheet: while the popular masses exhibited unmatched revolutionary courage, the sabotage of reactionary Arab elites, combined with the lack of a disciplined revolutionary vanguard, enabled British imperialism and Zionist settler-colonialism to crush the uprising.
