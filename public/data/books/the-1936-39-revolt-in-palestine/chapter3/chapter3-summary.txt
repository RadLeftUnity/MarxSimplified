Kanafani turns to the agrarian crisis, illustrating how the Palestinian peasantry (fellahin) formed the demographic backbone and most militant detachment of the anti-colonial resistance. Under the British Mandate, exorbitant colonial taxation, predatory usury, and the commodification of land drove thousands of peasant families into destitution and debt. Absentee feudal landlords (the effendi class) capitalized on rising land prices by selling vast tracts of fertile agricultural land directly to Zionist purchasing agencies, such as the Jewish National Fund. The resulting mass evictions severed the fellahin from their ancestral lands, transforming self-sustaining farmers into impoverished day laborers and shantytown dwellers around Haifa and Jaffa. Kanafani demonstrates that Sheikh Izz al-Din al-Qassam's militant rural organizing found immediate resonance among these displaced fellahin, transforming agrarian dispossession into armed anti-imperialist guerrilla warfare.
