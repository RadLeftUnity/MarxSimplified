In this introductory chapter, Ghassan Kanafani establishes the historical and materialist framework for analyzing the 1936-1939 Palestinian revolt. Rejecting superficial bourgeois narratives that treat the revolt as a mere spontaneous outbreak of communal friction or religious fanaticism, Kanafani emphasizes that the uprising was the culmination of acute socio-economic contradictions engineered by British imperialism and Zionist settler-colonialism. He defines the tripartite enemy confronting the Palestinian national movement: British imperialism as the colonial hegemon, Zionist colonization operating as an aggressive capitalist and settler project, and reactionary Arab feudal and compradore elites who subordinated the popular struggle to protect their class privileges. Kanafani outlines the methodological necessity of examining the distinct roles of the workers, peasants, and intellectuals in order to understand both the extraordinary heroism and the ultimate defeat of the uprising.
