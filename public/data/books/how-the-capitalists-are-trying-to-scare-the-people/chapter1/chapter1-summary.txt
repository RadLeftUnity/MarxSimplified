In this May 1917 article in Pravda, V.I. Lenin dismantles the alarmist rhetoric of the bourgeois financial press. Reacting to an editorial in Finansovaya Gazeta that warned of financial collapse and claimed that workers' control required stripping tens of millions of citizens of their property rights, Lenin exposes the blatant fallacies designed to scare the Russian populace away from revolutionary action.

Lenin explains that the February Revolution was already a social revolution that handed state power from feudal tsarist landlords to the capitalist bourgeoisie. Objective crises caused by the imperialist world war made workers' regulation of banks and syndicates an absolute necessity to prevent mass starvation and industrial ruin. Most crucially, Lenin clarifies that neither workers' control nor socialism proposes to dispossess the millions of small peasants and shopkeepers; rather, socialist revolution breaks the sabotage of only a few hundred monopoly millionaires and oligarchs.
