Bukharin analyzes the collapse of the Second International in 1914 through the lens of the world economy. Colonial superprofits extracted by dominant state-capitalist trusts enabled the bourgeoisie to bribe a privileged labor aristocracy and foster a temporary illusion of 'national solidarity' and class collaboration with the imperialist state. Yet the immense destruction, inflation, and slaughter of imperialist war inevitably shatter reformist illusions and regenerate revolutionary proletarian internationalism.
