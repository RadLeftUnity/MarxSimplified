Bukharin examines both the extensive growth of the world economy (its geographic spread into non-capitalist territories) and its intensive growth (the deepening network of international economic ties). Driven by rapid technical revolutions in electrical power transmission, internal combustion engines, chemical synthesis, turbines, oceanic shipping cables, and global railways, international trade, labor migration, and above all the export of finance capital through multinational banking syndicates bind the globe into an interdependent production organism.
