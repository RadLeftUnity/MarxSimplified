Bukharin synthesizes the three economic roots of capitalist expansion: the struggle for sales markets (overproduction of industrial goods), the struggle for raw material sources (underproduction of agricultural and extractive materials), and the struggle for spheres of capital investment (overaccumulation of capital). All three represent facets of a single fundamental contradiction: the growing conflict between the global socialization of productive forces in the world economy and the narrow 'national' state boundaries of bourgeois property.
