Applying Marx's distinction between the concentration and centralization of capital to the world economy, Bukharin shows how capital concentration progresses from individual enterprises to industrial trusts and finally to organized 'national economies' functioning as giant 'state-capitalist trusts'. Competitive centralization now takes the form of a struggle among rival state-capitalist trusts, which absorb industrial competitors horizontally and annex agrarian colonies vertically to form self-sufficient economic combines.
