Bukharin defines the world economy as a system of production relations and corresponding exchange relations on a global scale. He distinguishes between the natural prerequisites of the international division of labor (differences in climate, soils, and mineral deposits) and the social prerequisites arising from the uneven development of productive forces. Reproducing the domestic cleavage between town and country on a global scale, world capitalism divides nations into industrialized 'town' countries exporting manufactured goods and agrarian 'country' regions supplying raw materials and foodstuffs.
