In this comprehensive twelve-part address, Mao Zedong applies materialist dialectics to socialist society after the basic completion of the socialist transformation of ownership. Rejecting the naive notion that contradictions disappear under socialism, he establishes a foundational distinction between two types of social contradictions: antagonistic contradictions between the people and their enemies, and non-antagonistic contradictions among the people themselves. While the people's democratic dictatorship suppresses counter-revolutionaries and external aggressors, dictatorship cannot be applied within the ranks of the people. Instead, contradictions among workers, peasants, intellectuals, and state organs must be resolved through democratic centralism, persuasion, education, and the rectification formula of "unity, criticism, unity." Mao further examines agricultural cooperatives, the peaceful transformation of the national bourgeoisie, the remoulding of intellectuals, minority nationality relations, the policy of "letting a hundred flowers blossom and a hundred schools of thought contend," the dangers of dogmatism and revisionism, and China's path to socialist industrialization.
