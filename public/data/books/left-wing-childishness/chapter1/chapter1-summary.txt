Lenin examines the 'Theses on the Present Situation' published by the 'Left Communists' in their journal Kommunist in April 1918. He demonstrates that events since the signing of the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk have completely vindicated the Bolshevik majority: the peace treaty sharpened contradictions among the competing imperialist powers while securing a vital breathing space for the exhausted workers and peasants of Soviet Russia. By dismissing the masses' need for peace as an 'inactive peace mentality' and refusing to calculate the real balance of forces, the Left Communists exhibit the subjective idealism and aristocratic posturing of declassed petty-bourgeois intellectuals brandishing a cardboard sword.
