Turning to domestic economic policy, Lenin draws a fundamental distinction between simple confiscation (which requires only political determination) and genuine socialization (which requires the organizational ability to calculate, account for, and distribute production nationwide). He analyzes the five socio-economic structures intermingled in post-revolutionary Russia: patriarchal peasant farming, small commodity production, private capitalism, state capitalism, and socialism. Because Russia is predominantly a country of small commodity producers, the main internal enemy of socialism is not state capitalism, but the petty-bourgeois anarchy of small proprietors, hoarders, and grain profiteers who resist all state accounting and control.
