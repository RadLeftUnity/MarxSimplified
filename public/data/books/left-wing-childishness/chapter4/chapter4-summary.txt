Lenin illustrates the relationship between state capitalism and socialism through the historical contrast of 1918: Germany possessed the most advanced material and organizational apparatus of large-scale state-monopoly capitalism under a reactionary imperialist state, whereas Russia possessed the most advanced political power (the Soviet dictatorship of the proletariat) amidst economic backwardness. Because socialism is impossible without large-scale engineering and planned state organization, the Soviet state must utilize state-capitalist methods and national accounting as the essential material threshold leading to socialism, rather than abstractly counterposing 'capitalism' and 'socialism'.
