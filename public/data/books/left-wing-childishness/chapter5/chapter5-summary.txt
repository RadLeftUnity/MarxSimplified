Lenin engages Nikolai Bukharin's reference to Marx's observation that under specific conditions the working class could 'buy out' the bourgeoisie. Applied to Soviet Russia, where the proletariat holds state power but lacks technical expertise, the working class must suppress uncultured profiteers while paying high salaries to employ and learn from bourgeois technical specialists and trust organizers under strict workers' control. Finally, Lenin critiques Bukharin's review of The State and Revolution for focusing solely on smashing the old bourgeois state (a task shared with petty-bourgeois revolutionaries) while overlooking the constructive proletarian task of tomorrow: establishing strict workplace discipline, nationwide accounting, and state control over the measure of labor and consumption.
