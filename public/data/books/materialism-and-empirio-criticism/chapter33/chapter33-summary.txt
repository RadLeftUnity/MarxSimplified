Lenin analyzes the ideological struggle within English natural science, contrasting the materialist physics of Sir Arthur Rücker with the subjective idealism of Karl Pearson and the open spiritualism of James Ward. Rücker defended physical realism, arguing that atoms and electrons exist as objective physical realities. Ward, an idealist philosopher, gleefully seized upon Pearson and Mach to claim that physics had abandoned realism and was returning to God. Lenin demonstrates how idealist philosophy exploits scientific uncertainty to rehabilitate religious spiritualism.
