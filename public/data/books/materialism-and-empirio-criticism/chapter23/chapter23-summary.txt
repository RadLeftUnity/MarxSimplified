Lenin exposes the petty factional disputes among the Russian revisionists. Yushkevich, who called himself an empirio-symbolist, ridiculed Chernov for attempting to combine positivism with the agrarian populist philosophy of Mikhailovsky. Lenin shows that beneath their reciprocal insults and rival scholastic terminologies, both Chernov and Yushkevich were swimming in the identical muddy stream of subjective idealism and agnosticism, united in their hatred of consistent dialectical materialism.
