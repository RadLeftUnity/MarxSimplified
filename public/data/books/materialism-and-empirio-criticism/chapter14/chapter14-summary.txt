Lenin investigates the dialectical relationship between absolute truth and relative truth, refuting Bogdanov's accusation that Engels was eclectic. Lenin explains that human knowledge is historically relative, reflecting reality with varying degrees of completeness; however, every relative truth contains an element of absolute truth. Relativism as an epistemological foundation leads straight to subjective idealism and skepticism; dialectical materialism recognizes the relativity of our knowledge only in the sense of the inexhaustibility of the objective world and the historical limits of human approximation.
