Lenin investigates the philosophical controversies in France surrounding Henri Poincaré and Pierre Duhem. Both Poincaré and Duhem adopted conventionalist views, claiming that physical theories are merely convenient mathematical conventions and definitions rather than reflections of objective reality. Lenin shows how reactionary Catholic thinkers and fideists seized upon Poincaré's conventionalism to argue that science does not provide objective truth, and therefore religious dogma is just as legitimate as scientific law.
