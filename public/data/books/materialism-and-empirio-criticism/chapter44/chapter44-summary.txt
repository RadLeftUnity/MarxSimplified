In this supplementary essay, Lenin analyzes the materialist philosophy of the great Russian revolutionary democrat Nikolai Chernyshevsky. In his work The Aesthetic Relation of Art to Reality, Chernyshevsky criticized Kantianism not from the right (like the Machians and agnostics who rejected the thing-in-itself), but consistently from the left, affirming that external reality exists objectively outside human consciousness and is fully knowable. Lenin holds up Chernyshevsky as an inspiring Russian materialist thinker who remained completely immune to the idealist infection that later seduced the Russian Machians.
