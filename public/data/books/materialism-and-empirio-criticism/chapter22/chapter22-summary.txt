Lenin provides a masterclass in the history of philosophy by analyzing the dual critique of Immanuel Kant. Kant was an inconsistent dualist: on the left, materialists like Feuerbach and the French Enlightenment criticized Kant for his subjective apriorism and unknowable things-in-themselves, demanding that the thing-in-itself be recognized as knowable objective reality. On the right, Humeans and subjective idealists like Mach and the neo-Kantians criticized Kant for positing the existence of the thing-in-itself at all. The Machians attack Kant from the right, regressing into pure subjective idealism.
