Lenin examines Richard Avenarius's theory of the principal co-ordination, which asserts that the observing self (the central term) and the surrounding environment (the counter-term) are indissolubly linked and cannot be conceived apart. Lenin contrasts this scholastic dogma with naive realism: the spontaneous materialist conviction shared by all humanity and practicing natural scientists that the external world exists independently of any perceiving subject. Avenarius's co-ordination is merely subjective idealism wrapped in convoluted academic jargon.
