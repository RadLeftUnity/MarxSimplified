Lenin examines how the crisis of physics unfolded in Germany, contrasting the materialist physicist Ludwig Boltzmann with the idealist tendencies of Mach and Ostwald. Boltzmann tenaciously defended physical atomism, recognizing that physical models reflect real material structures existing in nature. Lenin shows that German neo-Kantians and idealists praised Mach not for his contributions to physics, but because his epistemology weakened scientific materialism and left room for German metaphysical idealism.
