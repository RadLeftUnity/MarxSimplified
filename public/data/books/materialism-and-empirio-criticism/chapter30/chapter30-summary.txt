Lenin opens his famous fifth chapter analyzing the philosophical crisis triggered by revolutionary breakthroughs in natural science at the turn of the twentieth century. The discoveries of radioactivity, X-rays, and the electron shattered the classical Newtonian model of the indivisible, billiard-ball atom. Unable to think dialectically, many physicists and philosophers fell into panic, concluding that because mechanical models had broken down, matter had disappeared and science had refuted materialism. Lenin undertakes an exhaustive survey of contemporary European physics to demonstrate that the crisis was purely epistemological.
