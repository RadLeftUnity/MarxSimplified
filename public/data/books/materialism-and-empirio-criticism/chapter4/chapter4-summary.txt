Lenin analyzes Ernst Mach's central philosophical proposition: that physical bodies are nothing but complexes of sensations. Drawing on the works of Marx and Engels, Lenin delineates the two irreconcilable fundamental trends in philosophy. Materialism proceeds from the external, objective world to human sensation and thought, regarding ideas as reflections of independent material reality. Idealism proceeds from human sensation and thought to external things, asserting that the physical world is constructed out of mental states. Mach's doctrine lands unequivocally in the idealist camp.
