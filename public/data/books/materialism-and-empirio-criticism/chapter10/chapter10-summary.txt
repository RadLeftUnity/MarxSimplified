Lenin defends Frederick Engels's materialist theory of knowledge against Socialist-Revolutionary leader Viktor Chernov and Russian Machians who attacked the concept of the thing-in-itself. Engels demonstrated that through human practice, industry, and experiment, things-in-themselves are continually converted into things-for-us. Lenin deduces three fundamental epistemological conclusions: things exist objectively independent of our consciousness; there is no unbridgeable boundary between the phenomenon and the thing-in-itself, only between what is known and what is not yet known; and knowledge develops dialectically from the incomplete to the complete.
