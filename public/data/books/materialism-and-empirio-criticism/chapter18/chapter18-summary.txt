Lenin tackles one of the most critical battlegrounds in the philosophy of science: causality and objective necessity in nature. Materialism recognizes that cause and effect, natural laws, and necessity exist objectively in physical nature, independent of the human mind. The idealist and agnostic school, from Hume and Kant to Mach and Pearson, denies objective causality, claiming that cause and effect are merely psychological habits, subjective associations, or mental rules imposed on nature by human reason. Lenin demonstrates that denying objective causality directly leads to religious miracles and fideism.
