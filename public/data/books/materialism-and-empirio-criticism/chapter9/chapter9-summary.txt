Lenin demonstrates the inevitable logical terminus of empirio-criticism: solipsism. If the world is merely my sensation, then consistency demands concluding that only I, the philosophizing individual, exist, while other people and the external universe are mere figments of my perception. Mach and Avenarius attempt to escape this absurdity by claiming that other people's sensations also exist, but Lenin points out they have no theoretical right to do so without abandoning their fundamental idealist premise that reality consists solely of immediate experience.
