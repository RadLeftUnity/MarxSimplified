Lenin contrasts the reception of Ernst Mach with that of the materialist zoologist Ernst Haeckel, whose book The Riddle of the Universe provoked furious condemnation from bourgeois theologians and idealist professors worldwide. Haeckel did not consider himself a philosopher, but his spontaneous, naive materialism in biology struck a devastating blow against religious superstition. While bourgeois professors showered Mach with praise for reconciling science with religion, they viciously attacked Haeckel, proving once again that philosophy in class society is partisan to the core.
