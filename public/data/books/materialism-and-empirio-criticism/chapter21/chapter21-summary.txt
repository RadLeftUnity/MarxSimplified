Lenin examines the dialectical relationship between freedom and necessity, taking issue with Lunacharsky's confused reading of Engels. Engels, developing Hegel's brilliant formulation, established that freedom does not consist in an imaginary independence from natural laws, but in the knowledge of these laws and the possibility of systematically using them for human purposes. Necessity is blind only so long as it is not understood; through scientific knowledge and revolutionary practice, necessity is transformed into conscious human freedom.
