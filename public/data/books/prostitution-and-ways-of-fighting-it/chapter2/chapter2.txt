The workers' revolution in Russia has shattered the basis of capitalism and struck a blow at the former dependence of women upon men. All citizens are equal before the work collective. A woman provides for herself not by marriage but by the part she plays in production and the contribution she makes to the people's wealth.

From the point of view of the national economy, the professional prostitute is a labour deserter. She is a person whose energy is not used for the collective; a person who lives off others. In the interests of the economy we must start an immediate fight to reduce the number of prostitutes and eliminate prostitution in all its forms.

We do not condemn prostitution and fight against it as a special moral category, but as an aspect of labour desertion. To us in the workers' republic it is not important whether a woman sells herself to one man or to many, whether she is classed as a professional prostitute selling her favours to a succession of clients or as a wife selling herself to her husband.

We cannot make a difference between a prostitute and a lawful wife kept by her husband. The workers' collective condemns the prostitute not because she gives her body to many men, but because, like the legal wife who stays at home and avoids socialized production, she does no useful work for the society. Both are labour deserters.

The Interdepartmental Commission opposed the suggestion that prostitutes be criminalised or thrown into forced labour camps simply for being prostitutes. Prostitutes are not treated any differently from other labour deserters; they are directed to social welfare networks, job placement, or hospitals, and only face labour obligations if they repeatedly refuse work.
