Published in December 1905 during the first Russian Revolution, this foundational essay by V.I. Lenin sets forth the Marxist materialist approach to religion and the state. Lenin explains that religion serves as an ideological instrument of class domination, functioning as spiritual oppression that preaches submission to workers while granting cheap moral absolution to exploiters.

Lenin makes a crucial dialectical distinction regarding the slogan that religion is a private affair. In relation to the state, religion must be strictly private: all state funding, church privileges, and official religious records must be eradicated, establishing total freedom of conscience and strict separation of church and state. However, in relation to the revolutionary party of the proletariat, religion is not a private affair. The party is founded on scientific materialism and actively propagates atheism and scientific education. At the same time, Lenin warns against bourgeois-idealist crusades that turn religion into an abstract obsession, stressing that the party welcomes religious workers because unity in the living class struggle on earth takes absolute precedence over theoretical disputes about heaven.
