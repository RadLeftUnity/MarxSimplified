In this January 1987 interview with TASS special envoy Boris Chabaev, President Thomas Sankara reflects on the historic 1986 Reykjavik summit between the Soviet Union and the United States, evaluating global peace initiatives through an anti-colonial and Pan-African lens. Sankara commends the unilateral peace initiatives presented by the Soviet leadership while rejecting the imperialist framework that reduces international diplomacy to great-power rivalries. He asserts that African nations are not passive pawns on a superpower chessboard: modern military aggression, ecological damage, and economic instability reverberate across the entire globe, granting African peoples a legitimate right and duty to intervene in global peace processes.

Sankara examines the role of the Organization of African Unity (OAU), noting that while various member states remain compromised by neocolonial ties, revolutionaries must maintain the courage to openly expose imperialist exploitation in international forums. He details the covert warfare used against African sovereignty, including foreign military surveillance centers, strategic enclaves, manufactured border conflicts, and armed counter-revolutionary militias dispatched to overthrow progressive governments. Sankara concludes by emphasizing that the primary mission of the Burkinabè Revolution is raising popular political consciousness across urban and rural populations alike: only by recognizing imperialism as the concrete, material root of underdevelopment can working people take hold of their own collective destiny and build a self-reliant socialist society.
